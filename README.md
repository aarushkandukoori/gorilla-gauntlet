# Gorilla Gauntlet

**How many copies of a public figure does it take to beat a silverback gorilla?**
A static, ad-ready web app: pick a figure (Ronnie Coleman, Mike Tyson, Brian Shaw, Khabib… or
import anyone from Wikidata), watch a physics-based fight play out with neural-network brains on
both sides, and let the app keep adding a copy until the humans win. A Monte Carlo solver reports
the reliable number.

Live: https://aarushkandukoori.github.io/gorilla-gauntlet/

No build step. Open `index.html` over http (the live trainer uses a Web Worker):

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8765 --directory .
```

## Features

- **Physics arena** (`js/env.js`): metres, kilograms, 9.81 m/s² gravity, 20 Hz. Force-driven
  motion with drag (real top speeds), circle collisions with momentum exchange, impulse-based
  hits that launch bodies ballistically, landing damage, knockdowns, trampling, and holders
  who ride the gorilla and load it with grip force plus dead weight.
- **RL brains** (`js/policy.js`, `js/es.js`, `train.js`): small MLP policies for the humans
  (27→32→32→8, one shared net that reads each figure's own stats) and the gorilla
  (24→32→32→7), trained by evolution-strategies policy search in alternating self-play.
  Shipped weights live in `js/brains.js`; you can keep training in the browser.
- **Scripted brains** as baselines/fallback: swarm & pin, mixed, strike only.
- **Roster of 25 figures** with public metrics (height, weight, documented lifts, fight records)
  and an editable 0–1 fight profile (striking, grappling, endurance, speed, toughness).
- **Wikidata import**: search any person; height (P2048), mass (P2067) and occupations (P106/P641)
  are pulled live and mapped to an archetype.
- **Animated fight scene** (`js/render.js`): procedurally drawn, articulated figures on a 3/4-view
  jungle clearing, driven directly by physics positions. Wind-ups, ballistic throws, chest-beating
  break-frees, dust, blood decals, damage pop-ups, camera shake, slow-motion finish, HUD.
- **Synthesized sound** (`js/audio.js`): thuds, bites, whooshes, roars, bell. `M` to mute.
- **Auto-escalation** — "Keep adding a copy until they win" reruns with N+1 after every loss.
- **Solve it** — 60 silent fights per crew size; smallest crew that wins ≥50% and ≥90%.
- **Ad slots**: leaderboard, sidebar rectangle, footer; Auto-ads mode with just a client id.
- Deep links: `?figure=mike-tyson&n=5`.

## The physics (plain English)

Bodies are circles with mass on a ground plane plus a height axis. Each actor applies a drive
force in a chosen direction, scaled by effort and fatigue, against linear drag tuned so the
terminal speed is its sprint speed (3.2 + 4.2 × speed m/s for humans; 3.5–9 m/s for the gorilla
depending on stamina). Bodies collide and exchange momentum; a charging gorilla tramples.

**Hits are impulses.** A gorilla swing delivers `J = (290 + 0.22 × strength) × U(0.75, 1.25)` N·s
(~440 N·s fresh) at 30° upward: a 90 kg man gets ~4.9 m/s and flies; a 200 kg strongman gets
~2.2 m/s. Damage = `0.03 × J²/(2m) + 0.06 × J`, scaled by toughness, so mass genuinely absorbs
blows. Landing faster than 2.5 m/s costs `0.018 × ½mv²` and floors you for a while. Bites reach
1 m, land 85% on a held or floored target but can be slipped by a free, fast fighter, are hampered
when two or more bodies hang on, and 12% of them are fight-ending. Human punches carry 15–50 N·s and mostly bounce
off (93% negated); an eye-rake (0.5–4% of landed punches, by skill) hurts and dazes.

**Holding.** A grab succeeds with probability from grappling skill, harder on a fresh or charging
gorilla. Holders ride the gorilla; their restraint
`(strength × (0.35 + 0.65 × grappling) + 0.5 × mass × (0.5 + 0.5 × grappling)) × fatigue`
subtracts (as grip force) from the gorilla's drive and adds 80% of their mass to what it must
move. When restraint ≥ effective strength and the gorilla is nearly stationary it is pinned; it
must "shake" — a burst `strength × (0.9 + U × (0.2 + 0.6 × √stamina))` — to hurl 1–3 holders
(~350 N·s each) at a stamina cost. Effective strength is
`815 × (floor + (1 − floor) × stamina^0.7) × (0.6 + 0.4 × health)` with the floor decaying from
0.40 to 0.20 after 90 s at redline. Humans win after 10 s of pin with gorilla stamina < 30%
(or gorilla HP 0); the gorilla wins when everyone is down or at the 3-minute cap. A hold is
sticky: only a deliberate retreat/rest lets go, a holder can still punch (weaker) from the hold, and
swings (60%) or bites (70%) knock holders off.

## The brains

Every 0.2 s each actor observes a normalized vector and picks one discrete action.

| Actor | Observation (dims) | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human | direction/distance/closing speed to gorilla, in-reach flags, gorilla facing & wind-up aimed at me, own HP/stamina/holding/floored, gorilla HP/stamina/pinned/hampered, holders, allies near/down, own speed, nearest ally, own striking/grappling/endurance/speed/mass/strength (27) | sprint, advance, retreat, circle CW, circle CCW, strike, grab/hold, rest |
| Gorilla | nearest 3 humans (direction, distance, holding, floored), holders, humans near, standing, own stamina/HP/pinned/hampered/speed/dazed (24) | advance, charge, swing, bite, shake free, retreat, rest |

Policies are MLPs with tanh hidden layers; actions are argmax of the logits. Training is
OpenAI-ES-style: 32 antithetic Gaussian perturbation pairs (σ = 0.08), rank-shaped fitness,
Adam (lr 0.03), light weight decay, common random numbers across a generation (same figures,
crew sizes and seeds for every candidate). Sides alternate every 5 generations; the opponent is
the other side's current policy (50%), a snapshot from a short archive (20%, avoids cycling), or a
scripted policy (30%, so the gorilla still meets pure grapplers and the humans still meet a
gorilla that shakes off pins). Scenarios draw a random roster figure and N ∈ [3, 16], 90 s cap.

Reward (humans, team): `±1` for win/loss `+ 0.25 × gorilla stamina drained + 0.25 × gorilla damage
− 0.35 × casualties/N − 0.15 × damage taken + 0.25 × (1 − t/limit)` on a win. The gorilla's is the
mirror image with a bonus for finishing fast.

```bash
node train.js 400                       # trains (or resumes from js/brains.js) and writes js/brains.js
ONLY_SIDE=gorilla node train.js 100     # catch-up phase for one side only (also: ONLY_SIDE=human)
node crosseval.js 30                    # RL vs scripted brains, both sides, at fixed crew sizes
node mkcalib.js 30                      # writes CALIBRATION.md
node calibrate.js 30 [--heuristic]      # N50 / N90 ladder in the terminal
```

The shipped weights went through ~1,600 generations in several passes while the physics was being
tuned (each pass resumes from the previous weights), finishing with a gorilla-only catch-up phase.

The **Train the brains live** panel runs the identical trainer in a Web Worker at a smaller
scale and lets you swap the freshly trained weights in.

## Turning on ads

1. Get an AdSense (or other network) account approved for your domain.
2. In `js/ads.js` set `enabled: true` and your `client` id (`ca-pub-…`). With no slot ids this
   runs **Auto ads**. For fixed placements also fill one slot id per placement.
3. Put the `ads.txt` line AdSense gives you at the site root.

## Deploying

Any static host works (GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, S3). Push the folder as-is.
Wikidata import needs outbound HTTPS from the visitor's browser; nothing else calls out.

## Caveats

Everything here is a model, not a measurement. Gorilla strength is genuinely uncertain (4–9× an
adult man is the usual range), fight profiles are editorial estimates, and real gorillas mostly
bluff-charge and leave. Wild gorillas are endangered: https://gorillafund.org
